A. Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)

Turns 5 years of forecast free cash flow into today's enterprise value, equity value and value per share.

Free cash flow forecast ($M)
How this works

1. Each year's cash flow is divided by (1 + WACC)^year to get its present value (its value in today's money).

2. Terminal value (Gordon growth) = Year-5 FCF × (1 + g) / (WACC − g). This is also discounted back 5 years.

3. Enterprise value = sum of discounted cash flows + discounted terminal value.

4. Equity value = enterprise value − net debt. Value per share = equity value ÷ shares.

Cash flows are assumed to arrive at the end of each year.

B. WACC (Weighted Average Cost of Capital)

The discount rate for a DCF: the blended return that shareholders and lenders expect.

Cost of equity
How this works

Cost of equity (CAPM) = Risk-free rate + Beta × Market risk premium.

Weights: equity weight = E / (E + D), debt weight = D / (E + D).

After-tax cost of debt = Cost of debt × (1 − Tax rate), because interest reduces taxes.

WACC = Equity weight × Cost of equity + Debt weight × After-tax cost of debt.

How this works

For each type of multiple, the calculator finds the lowest, median (middle value) and highest peer multiple.

Implied enterprise value = Target metric × Multiple, for example $80M EBITDA × 9.75x = $780M.

With an even number of peers, the median is the average of the two middle values. The median is used because a single unusual peer affects it less than it affects the average.

D. Synergies & Offer Price

What's the most a buyer should pay, and who keeps the value created by the deal?

How this works

Risk-adjusted synergies = Synergies × Probability.

Net synergies = Risk-adjusted synergies − Integration costs. This is the value the deal creates.

Maximum sensible price = Standalone value + Net synergies. Paying more than this destroys value for the buyer.

Offer price = Standalone value × (1 + Premium). The premium goes to the seller. Whatever is left of the net synergies stays with the buyer.

E. Accretion / Dilution (simple)

Does buying the target raise (accretive) or lower (dilutive) the buyer's earnings per share in year 1?

How this works

Buyer net income = Buyer EPS × Buyer shares.

Stock part: new shares issued = Price × % stock ÷ Buyer share price.

Cash part is borrowed: after-tax interest = Price × % cash × Interest rate × (1 − Tax rate).

Pro forma EPS = (Buyer net income + Target net income − After-tax interest) ÷ (Buyer shares + New shares).

If pro forma EPS is above the buyer's current EPS, the deal is accretive. Remember: accretive does not automatically mean the deal creates value. This simple version also ignores fees, synergies and lost interest on cash.
